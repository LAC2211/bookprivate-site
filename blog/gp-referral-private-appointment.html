Do I need a GP referral for a private appointment?

Published 19 January 2026 · Explainer

This is one of the most common questions we hear, and it's usually followed by relief when people learn the answer. Short version: for most private healthcare services, no, you don't need a GP referral. You can book directly.

Services you can usually self-refer for

Private physiotherapy is almost always self-referral — you can book an appointment directly with a chartered physiotherapist without seeing your GP first. The same is true for most MSK (musculoskeletal) diagnostic scans and private ADHD assessments. Practitioners are trained to assess you, decide what's appropriate, and refer you onward themselves if something more serious needs investigating.

When a referral is genuinely needed

Some private consultant appointments — particularly certain surgical specialities — do still require a GP referral letter, partly for clinical safety and partly because private medical insurance policies often require it to process a claim. If you're paying privately without insurance, this requirement is far less common, but always worth checking with the specific clinic before booking.

Why this matters if you're on an NHS waiting list

A lot of people delay seeking private treatment because they assume they'll need to go through their GP first, adding another wait on top of the one they're already trying to avoid. In reality, for services like physiotherapy and ADHD assessment, you can typically book directly and be seen within days rather than months.
